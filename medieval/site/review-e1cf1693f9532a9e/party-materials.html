🕯️ Ravenmoor Feast: print cards Phase 2 Evidence Reward slips Reviewer page Host guide
How to print: Letter paper, landscape, default margins, scale 100%, Background graphics ON. Phase 2 cards print two to a sheet, evidence cards four to a sheet, reward slips eight to a sheet. Cut along the card borders. The invitation, Phase 1 pages and Phase 2 cards are digital (the Phase 2 card unlocks on each player's page with the passphrase the host reveals); these printed Phase 2 cards are an optional fallback. Evidence cards and reward slips are physical. Keep them sealed until the host's signal.

The Ghost of Ravenmoor

Baron Godfrey Aldous de Ravenmoor, now deceased
Player: Zach Johnson
Phase 2
Ghost

Your new role

  • You are DEAD. You are now the Ghost of Ravenmoor. Put on a sheet or a pale veil, if you have one, and wander the hall silently and mournfully. Do not speak, except to answer questions.
  • Any living guest may ask you yes-or-no questions, but you may answer only THREE in total across the whole evening. Answer by nodding or shaking your head. Keep count aloud in a hollow ghostly whisper: 'One...' (you still count everything).
  • Answer truthfully using the list below. If asked something not on the list, shake your head sadly (you do not know).
  • You still carry your own secret from your Phase 1 card, but you may only share it through a nod when asked a direct question about it.

Your truthful answers (use only if asked)

Was the poison in the Loving Cup? Yes.
Was it ratsbane? Yes.
Did you see who put it there? No. It came as a surprise to you.
Was the cup clean when it was tasted at a quarter past five? Yes.
Was the Great Hall empty between a quarter past five and the six o'clock bell? Yes. The main door was barred.
Was the cellar's own rat poison used? No.
Did you plan to speak of money owed at the toast? Yes. You meant to name three debtors.
Do you suspect the Queen of Cathay? No.

Reminder

You do not know who killed you. You only know you drank from the Cup. Enjoy haunting.

You win by watching them all fumble.

Queen Zhao Suyin of Cathay

Phase 2: The Baron is dead!
Player: Alyssum Johnson
Phase 2
Your Character

Your afternoon

You arrived at one o'clock. From half past four until the six o'clock bell you sat in the East Gallery taking tea with Master Crowe, who talked the entire time. Through the window you watched the tilt-yard, where Sir Cedric and his squire practised for hours. You counted seven pots of tea and forty-one passes at the quintain.

What else you know

At the Baron's request you were given a copy of the Order of Ceremonies for the evening, so that you would know the order of toasts. You found the Baron's habit of counting the minutes strangely comforting. The Baron's strange mixture of pride and fear troubled you; you think he was frightened of something.

Your new secret to trade: ABOUT CROWE: he never stops talking, but he did not leave your sight between half past four and six. You can vouch for him, and for Sir Cedric and his squire (you watched the whole tilt-yard).

Your evidence

You were handed a piece of evidence: The Baron's Order of Ceremonies. You may show it, describe it, or trade it for secrets or for other evidence. Whoever holds it can read it.

Your quest

Seek: Master Crowe's ledger. You want to know what a western merchant charges. Find his ledger of sales and loans and turn it in to the host. (The host will give you a Bonus Truth.)

Your goal

Work out WHO killed the Baron, HOW, and WHY. At the end of the party, write your accusation on a slip and hand it to the host. Remember: you may even be wrong about everyone, including yourself.

Host Reward Slips

When a guest turns in the evidence they were told to find, give them one slip (any one). Cut apart and keep face down.

Bonus Truth 1

The kitchen boy who tasted the Loving Cup at a quarter past five swore it was sound: nothing but spiced mead.

Bonus Truth 2

The Baron's own rat poison, kept under the Steward's lock, was untouched. The poison came from outside the keep.

Bonus Truth 3

A trial tray of violet comfits stood by the kitchen door at five o'clock. When the cook returned, it was lighter by a fistful.

Bonus Truth 4

A whole packet of ratsbane was stirred into the Cup, enough to give the mead a faint smell of garlic and iron.

Bonus Truth 5

The garden door's latch was found lifted at six o'clock, though the Steward swears he left it latched.

Bonus Truth 6

The Hall's main door stayed barred from a quarter past five until the six o'clock bell. The only way in was the garden door.

Bonus Truth 7

Fresh scuffs marked the gravel by the garden door: a shoe worn thin at the sole.

Bonus Truth 8

The Baron laughed on the stairs this morning: 'By All Souls' Day I shall own a fine new manor!'

Bonus Truth 9

The Loving Cup is the only silver goblet in the keep engraved with a raven. The Baron forbade anyone else to drink from it.

Bonus Truth 10

Ratsbane stirred into warm, spiced mead leaves no colour at all, only a gritty sweetness underneath the spice.

Bonus Truth 11

The kitchen boy who tasted the Cup was sent to the cellar for a keg at twenty past five, and saw no one in the passage to the Hall.

Bonus Truth 12

The Marshal swears that neither the knight nor his squire left the lists between half past four and the six o'clock bell.

Bonus Truth 13

The chapel's seven vigil candles were burned down to matching stubs by six o'clock. Whoever kept the vigil never left.
