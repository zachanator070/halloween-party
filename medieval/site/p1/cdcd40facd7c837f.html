Brother Ambrose Tuck Marlowe

Friar and Chaplain of Ravenmoor Chapel
Phase 1
Your Character

Who you are

You are the jolly, round chaplain of Ravenmoor, a friar who loves a good meal, a good laugh, and a very good ale. You are a gifted herbalist and healer and you know more about plants than anyone for twenty miles. You are rarely entirely sober.

Your quirks (lean into them!)

You bless everything ('Benedicite, soup!') in terrible, made-up Latin ('Dominus pass-the-salt-um!'), burst into hiccups when nervous, and tell long stories that wander off into recipes. You offer penance in food form ('Three pies and a cheese!').

How you treat others

  • Be warm and cheerful to everyone, and bless everything.
  • Scold the Squire gently for his sins (clumsiness).
  • Be an ally to the Steward, but needle him about the keys.
  • Fuss over anyone who looks ill or sad.

Your relationships

  • Lady Rosamund Thornbury: One of your flock. She confesses little, but you see her sorrows.
  • Godwin Hobsworth, the Steward: Your drinking buddy and fellow cellar-raider.
  • Baron Godfrey: He lets you live in the chapel and complains about your tankard.

Your flaw

Gluttony and tipsiness. You will say anything for another sip, and you sometimes fall asleep in the middle of a blessing.

Your secret to trade: ABOUT STEWARD GODWIN: he sneaks sips of the Baron's best wine from the cellar every night. Cask 9 is nearly empty. Trade this for information.
You may reveal it only in exchange for information. You may bluff, but you may not show anyone this page.

Props to bring

  • A brown robe or blanket tied with a rope belt
  • A string of prayer beads (or a necklace)
  • A tankard (a mug)
  • A small book or 'herb journal'

Costume tip: A brown hooded robe with a rope belt, sandals, a bit of flour on your sleeves.

Gift to bring for the Baron: A bottle of 'blessed' herbal tonic (it tastes like feet) and a loaf of fresh bread.

Conversation starters

  • 'Benedicite! Is this soup or a sacrament?'
  • 'Child, what ails you? Have you tried more cheese?'
  • 'Hic! I only had a thimble of mead. A large thimble.'
Keep this page secret. More will be revealed at the feast.
