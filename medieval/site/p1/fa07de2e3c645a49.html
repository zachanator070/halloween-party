Baron Godfrey Aldous de Ravenmoor

Lord of Ravenmoor Keep, your gracious host
Phase 1
Your Character

Prepared for Zach Johnson. Keep this page secret!

Who you are

You are the proud, slightly anxious master of Ravenmoor, a modest barony you describe as 'practically a kingdom'. Tonight's feast is your grandest ever. A foreign queen has honoured your hall with a visit and you mean to impress her, charm the King, and sign a trade charter. You know almost nothing about the queen except that she has come from very far away with an escort and a great many trunks. Pretend you know more.

Your quirks (lean into them!)

You COUNT everything aloud as you go ('Seven candles! Splendid!', 'Four guests by the fire, a fine number!') and you introduce every guest with an absurd, grand title you invent on the spot ('The Duke of Sunsets', 'Her Grace the Countess of Pudding'). You are certain your cellar is the finest in the shire.

How you treat others

  • Greet everyone as the most honoured guest you have ever had, then forget their name and invent a new title.
  • Flatter the King and Queen constantly and fuss that they have the best chairs.
  • Hover around the foreign queen and beg her for stories of her homeland, then nod as if you already knew.
  • Whenever money is mentioned, change the subject to your cellar.

Your relationships

  • Lord Reginald Thornbury: Your oldest friend from boyhood, and also, awkwardly, a debtor. You have been patient. Tonight patience runs out.
  • Master Bartholomew Crowe: Your business partner in lending and trade. You suspect he cheats you, but you need him.
  • King Oswin: Your liege. His approval is your favourite food.

Your flaw

Vanity. You cannot bear to be corrected, and you will bluff rather than admit you don't know something.

Your secret to trade: ABOUT MASTER CROWE: his famous 'Eastern silk' is dyed English wool. You have known for months and have said nothing because you profit too. Trade this for information.
You may reveal it only in exchange for information. You may bluff, but you may not show anyone this page.

Props to bring

  • A big ring of keys (any keys!)
  • A goblet or fancy mug to wave around
  • A rolled paper 'guest list' you consult and ignore
  • A cape: a bedsheet or bathrobe with a pinned-on brooch

Costume tip: A velvet-looking robe or bathrobe, a belt, fur trim from a scarf, a slightly crooked small crown or cap.

Gift to bring for the Baron: A little engraved souvenir spoon for each guest (they are slightly bent).

Conversation starters

  • 'Have you seen my cellar? Four hundred casks!' (it is not four hundred.)
  • 'Did you count the candles? Eleven!'
  • 'Tell me, what do you know of our esteemed guest?'
